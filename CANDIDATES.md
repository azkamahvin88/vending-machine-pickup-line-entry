# Candidate Pickup Lines — Vending Machine Contest

Brief: one pickup line, max 200 characters, spoken AS a vending machine
flirting with a passing human. Judge criteria, in order:
1. Machine voice — does it really sound like the machine talking?
2. Machine world — slots, buttons, coils, exact change, dispensing, etc.
3. Funny
4. Short and easy to say out loud
5. Charming, not creepy

Scores are 1–5 per criterion (max 25).

## Scoring notes

- **#1 (23, winner):** "exact change" is the single most vending-machine phrase
  there is, and "adds up" does double duty (money math + feelings). The
  machine clearly speaks in first person ("in me"), the line is one breath
  long, and it flatters without touching, following, or implying anything
  physical. Strongest on the judge's #1 and #2 criteria combined with a
  clean, warm joke.
- **#2/#3/#5 (22):** all solid, but each leans on one trick only — #2 is
  sweet but the joke is thin, #3's "press me" risks reading suggestive
  (criterion 5), #5 is charming but barely sounds like a machine speaking
  (criterion 1).
- **#4 (22):** great machine voice ("Insert coin to continue") but at 103
  chars it is the longest and loses speakability points.
- **#6/#7/#12:** coil/button jokes land, but "wound up and ready to drop"
  and "stuck on you like chips" tilt toward the machine malfunctioning
  rather than flirting, and #12's self-referential setup is wordy.
- **#8/#9/#10:** sweet, but the machine-world vocabulary is thin (#8) or
  the line reads more like a fortune-cookie than a machine (#10).
- **#11:** the funniest beep, but almost no machine-world substance beyond
  the beep itself, and "BEEP" alone is not charming.
- **#13/#14:** decent, but #13 blames the human for a malfunction (mildly
  creepy-adjacent) and #14 is meta about the line itself rather than
  flirting.

## Final pick

> Are you exact change? Because every time you walk by, something in me finally adds up.

86 characters. One line, no newline. Machine voice, exact-change world,
short, funny, charming.
